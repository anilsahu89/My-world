Summary (2 lines)

The cleanest public walkthrough of a professional order-flow scalping model: trade only when the market is out of balance (auction-market-theory framing), refine location with low-volume nodes, trigger on big-order aggression bubbles, target the previous point of control and take *full* profit there (~70% reversal odds). Plus a second mean-reverting model for balanced sessions, and a session-split (directional New York / mean-reverting London) with a disciplined "be wrong immediately" risk culture.

The three-step directional model (New York session)

1. LOCATION (profile): markets spend most time *in balance* (value area). Trend patterns inside balance continue only ~30% of the time — that's the stop-out machine killing retail. Wait for out-of-balance conditions; he claims this alone lifts win rate 20–30 points.

2. VALIDATION (structure + inefficiency): market structure/supply-demand zones; inefficiency (= one side's aggression, the real meaning behind "fair value gap"); low-volume nodes as reaction and continuation levels. Alert placed just below the node — *not* a resting limit order.

3. TRIGGER (order flow): a big-order bubble (aggressive prints) at the location. "With direction + location + aggression your ability to predict is zero but your ability to read is 100." Stop goes immediately beyond the bubble → tiny risk; target = the previous balance area / POC.

Exit rule worth stealing: take the full position off at the previous balance area — the market reverses there ~70% of the time, so holding for the extra 30% is negative-EV. (Contrast: our systems' runner halves and 15:00 square-offs.)

Model 2 — mean-reverting (balanced sessions / London)

  • In consolidation, price oscillates around value: buy deep discount relative to the volume distribution, target the POC (where maximum volume traded = where operators transact).
  • Skip the first breakout, take the second swing: first break → wait for the retracement → big-buy bubble → enter with the big hands. First attempts fail 7-of-10 at the NASDAQ open.
  • "If I'm wrong, I want to be wrong immediately" — never widen a stop against position.

Session & risk philosophy

  • Session-split models: directional model only in the NY session (first London hour = fakeouts); mean-reversion for balanced/London conditions.
  • "Building profit for the day": daily accumulation, stop at "enough" ("1,000 is enough — tomorrow is another day, I wait for the explosion day"). Goal = breakeven or small green on off-model days.
  • Scaling out when a squeeze appears (aggressive opposite flow after extension).
  • RR choice changes win rate: the further beyond daily ATR you target, the lower the probability.
