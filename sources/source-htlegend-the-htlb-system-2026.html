Summary (2 lines)

The fullest single disclosure of the "sacred" system: its backbone is the HTLB Tracker — mapping ₹100 Cr+ investors' accounts to bulk/block deals to detect their buys within ~24h — combined with a relative-strength comparison chart (stock vs CNX 100) and the 68-parameter HTLB Delta. The investment rulebook is now fully specified (¼-capital stacked entries, book 50% at +100%, breakeven at +5%, runner held until the tracked investor's stake drops below 1.5%), plus the swing instrument's first hard number: ~7% carry if the price sits still. Results (302% p.a. / 0.57% DD / RR 1:1276) remain self-scored.

The origin thesis (why they claim it works)

  • Named big investors (Kedia ~500%/5y, Jhunjhunwalas, "Mukul" ~1900%/5y…) average ~100% p.a. on ₹100 Cr+ books with tiny drawdowns — via small/midcaps ("a ₹500 Cr company can 10x; a ₹50,000 Cr one can't double") and access to future fundamentals: promoter connectivity, board meetings, order books.
  • HTLB's pitch: you can't get their information, but you can follow their money within 24 hours (bulk deals + account mapping) instead of waiting for quarterly shareholding data.

The HTLB investment rulebook (now complete)

1. Universe: small/midcaps where tracked investors accumulate (via the tracker).

2. HTLB Delta green — 68 data points aggregated from 16 engines/platforms into one metric score vs threshold (equity params vs futures params differ).

3. HTLB1 green + rising + above dotted HTLB2 on the stock-vs-CN X-100 comparison chart = "super-bullish zone." Doctrine: institutions always use comparison charts; single-counter trend lines are never used.

4. Entry: HTLB Delta forms a pullback "bent"; reference line at the peak; enter on the break (alert placed at prior close, entered intraday).

5. Sizing: partial entries — ¼ of capital per trade, stacked up to 3 entries (e.g., ₹10L × 3 on a ₹40L book).

6. Target +100%: book 50% (full investment out), hold the rest — "profits kept in the stock grow better than in the bank" (T+1 withdrawals anytime).

7. Breakeven at +5% (investment version; the swing version uses +1.5% price).

8. Stop: daily close below the reference line with a bearish sign → exit that tranche.

9. Runner exit (NEW, concrete): hold the remaining half until the tracked investor's stake falls below 1.5% — then exit fully.

Test claims & the swing instrument

  • 200+ counters × 4–5 years each = "1,000+ years of testing": avg ROI 302% p.a., avg Max DD 0.57%, avg RR 1:1276; capital 5× in ~1.5y, 10× in 2.25–5y; claims to beat the tracked investors themselves "3–4× growth, 100–200× safer."
  • "Never buy futures" (RR 1:1, carry cost) — replaced by a fully hedged option combination: profits on the target move (+3.5% price ≈ 20% ROI), jackpot on gap-downs (IV doubles), and — the first hard number — ~7% carry "interest paid back to you" if the price sits still. Strike selection: still gated ("trainings").
  • Swing money plan restated: 1 Cr → ₹20L equities → pledge (~80%) → ₹16L swing margin; 50% of each win banked, 50% re-pledged; losses tracked in points and recovered via the same trade-sequence escalation (already rejected in this thread).

Claims vs evidence

  • The tracker edge is plausible but not novel or private — bulk/block deals are public disclosures; "follow the big investor" products exist. The claimed 24h account-mapping is unverifiable.
  • Survivorship: the named ₹100 Cr+ investors are the survivors; the base rate of bulk-deal-following across all investors is not shown.
  • 302%/0.57% DD/1:1276 over overlapping histories, self-scored — same verdict as before: rejected as evidence.
  • The ₹1.5%-stake runner exit is genuinely interesting: an external, fundamentals-anchored exit — though retail sees holdings quarterly, which lags his claimed tracker.

What's genuinely adoptable

  • The comparison-chart doctrine (never judge a stock without its index-relative trend) — same family as htl rotation and NK's outperformance doctrine; already our test-E core.
  • The stacked partial entries (¼ × 3) — cleaner than single-shot position building; matches our patient-entry backtest lesson.
  • The 1.5%-stake external exit — worth testing with public shareholding data as a runner-management rule.
