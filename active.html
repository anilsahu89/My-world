Arbitrage 2.0 (Future Arbitrage V2)

Definition

Arbitrage 2.0 is Rita's filtered F1/F2 backwardation strategy, now codified in backtest_future_arbitaage.py. It trades only one structure:

Spot > Current-month future > Next-month future

Action: Buy current-month future, sell next-month future. Profit comes from the current-month future converging toward spot before expiry.

Do Not Mix

This structure is NOT Arbitrage 2.0:

Spot > Current-month future < Next-month future

That is a separate calendar-credit setup and must be tracked separately. Arbitrage 2.0 requires both futures below spot AND current future above next future.

Entry Rules (code-verified)

Enter one lot only when ALL pass (passes_entry_rules, backtest_future_arbitaage.py:207):

  1. curr.close < spot — current future below spot
  2. nxt.close < curr.close — next future below current future
  3. basis >= 10 — spot−current gap ≥ ₹10
  4. basis_pct >= 0 — (default off; set via --min-basis-pct)
  5. spread <= basis × 1.0 — current−next gap ≤ spot−current gap
  6. spread_value >= 2000 — one-lot spread value ≥ ₹2,000
  7. curr.volume >= 100 — current future volume ≥ 100 contracts
  8. nxt.volume >= 100 — next future volume ≥ 100 contracts
  9. min_days_to_expiry <= DTE <= max_days_to_expiry — (default 0–999 = no filter)

Where: - basis = spot − curr.close - spread = curr.close − nxt.close - spread_value = spread × curr.lot - basis_pct = basis / spot × 100

Ranking (when multiple stocks qualify)

Candidates are sorted descending by (basis_spread_ratio, basis, −spread): 1. basis_spread_ratio = basis / spread — highest first (want basis much larger than spread) 2. Then by basis (highest first) 3. Then by −spread (lowest spread first)

Trade Action

Leg Action
Current-month future Buy
Next-month future Sell

Exit Rules

Exit when either occurs (backtest_future_arbitaage.py:271): 1. curr.close >= spot — current future matches or crosses spot 2. day >= pos.expiry — current-month expiry reached 3. forced_end_of_backtest — final backtest day force-close

No separate price SL — the exit rule IS the stop. This is intentional: the structure itself defines risk.

Arbitrage 2.1 (Winning-Rate Variant)

Stricter filters for higher win rate (fewer trades). From ARBITAGE_2_0_RULES.md:

  • Spot−current gap ≥ ₹20
  • Spot−current gap ≥ 2× current−next gap
  • Spot−current gap ≥ 1.5% of spot
  • Next-month volume ≥ 500 contracts
  • Prefer 7–21 days before current-month expiry

Skip if current−next gap > 50% of spot−current gap.

  • Spread value too small to cover costs

Averaging Rules (from ARBITAGE_2_0_RULES.md)

Do NOT average blindly. Only add if ALL true: - Current future still below spot - Spot−current basis still healthy - Spread has widened from entry - Both legs still liquid - Total margin exposure acceptable

Avoid averaging when current future is close to spot OR spread has already compressed sharply.

BB Trap Positional (Daily Timeframe) — v2 Optimized

Definition

A positional reversal strategy on the daily timeframe combining: a full candle outside the Bollinger Band, a rejection-wick confirmation candle, and volume expansion. Designed to catch multi-day moves from band extremes.

Setup Rules — v2 Optimized (Backtested July 2026)

The original v1 rules (alert candle closes beyond primary extreme) are deprecated — backtesting showed they identify trends, not traps. The v2 rules below use a rejection wick confirmation instead.

SHORT Setup — THE PRIMARY EDGE (PF 2.10 with filters)

Step 1: Primary Candle (Candle 1, day t-2)

A daily candle where the ENTIRE candle is outside the upper Bollinger Band: - Low > Upper BB — the candle's lowest point never touched the band

Step 2: Alert Candle (Candle 2, day t-1) — REJECTION WICK

  • Upper wick ≥ 50% of the alert candle's total range
  • Formula: (High − max(Open, Close)) / (High − Low) ≥ 0.50
  • This means price pushed higher intraday but got rejected — the actual trap moment

Step 3: Volume Confirmation

  • vol_a ≥ 1.5× vol_p (alert candle volume must be 1.5× primary candle volume)

Step 4: RSI Filter (optional but recommended — lifts PF 1.41 → 2.10)

  • RSI(14) > 70 at signal day

Step 5: Entry (day t)

  • Enter SHORT at day t's close
  • SL = entry + (primary_range × 0.30) — 30% of primary candle range above entry
  • Target = entry − (primary_range × 0.80) — 80% of primary candle range below entry
  • Risk:Reward = 2.7×
  • Time stop = 10 trading days

LONG Setup — MARGINAL EDGE (PF 1.06, trade with caution)

Step 1: Primary Candle (Candle 1, day t-2)

  • High < Lower BB — entire candle below lower band

Step 2: Alert Candle (Candle 2, day t-1) — REJECTION WICK

  • Lower wick ≥ 50% of alert candle's total range
  • Formula: (min(Open, Close) − Low) / (High − Low) ≥ 0.50

Step 3: Volume (MANDATORY)

  • vol_a ≥ 1.5× vol_p (without this, longs lose money)

Step 4: Entry (day t)

  • Enter LONG at day t's close
  • SL = entry − (primary_range × 0.50)
  • Target = entry + (primary_range × 1.00)
  • Risk:Reward = 2.0×
  • Time stop = 10 trading days

Exit Rules

Target 1 (Primary): 20 SMA (Daily)

  • The 20 SMA is the Bollinger Band center line — price is magnetically attracted to it
  • Exit 50-70% of position at 20 SMA
  • This is the highest-probability target — most reversal moves reach the mean

Target 2 (Stretch): Opposite Bollinger Band

  • If momentum is strong, price can ride all the way to the opposite band
  • Shorts: ride to lower BB
  • Longs: ride to upper BB
  • Trail the remaining 30-50% with a trailing SL below/above recent swing highs/lows

Stop Loss

  • Shorts: SL = high of Candle 2 (the alert candle) + buffer
  • Longs: SL = low of Candle 2 (the alert candle) − buffer
  • If Candle 3 closes beyond Candle 2's extreme in the wrong direction → exit immediately

Time Stop

  • If price hasn't reached 20 SMA within 10 trading days → consider exiting (dead trade)
  • Opportunity cost of holding a position that isn't moving

Bollinger Band Parameters

  • Period: 20 (standard)
  • Standard deviations: 2 (standard)
  • MA type: SMA (simple moving average)
  • These are the defaults NK uses and what most charting platforms apply

Why This Should Work (the logic)

  1. Extreme deviation: A full daily candle entirely outside the 2-sigma band is a 2+ standard deviation event — statistically rare, mean reversion is highly probable
  2. Trap confirmation: Candle 2 closing further beyond candle 1's extreme = late buyers/sellers piling in = they're the trap targets
  3. Volume validation: Volume 2 > Volume 1 = smart money is active (per VPA "effort vs result"). Without volume, the move may be low-conviction noise — skip it
  4. Target logic: 20 SMA is the gravitational center — price reverts to the mean. Opposite band is the stretch target for strong moves
  5. Daily timeframe = positional: Daily signals are more reliable than intraday (less noise), and the moves last days-to-weeks (per BB Blast duration table: daily = 5-15 days effect)

What This Strategy is NOT

  • ❌ Not a BB Blast trade — BB Blast requires a PRIOR SQUEEZE (narrow bands) then breakout. This strategy trades the REVERSAL from band extremes, not the continuation
  • ❌ Not intraday — entries are on daily close, holds are multi-day. Don't use this on 15-min or 1-hour charts (different dynamics)
  • ❌ Not a Moh Maya variant — Moh Maya requires VWAP reclaim confirmation. This strategy uses the 2-candle + volume structure instead

Risk Management

Position Sizing

  • Max 1-2 positional trades at a time (daily TF signals are infrequent — maybe 2-4 per month per stock)
  • Risk per trade: ≤ 2% of capital (SL distance × quantity ≤ 2% of portfolio)
  • Since SL = Candle 2's extreme, calculate quantity AFTER identifying the setup

Scenario Handling

Scenario Action
Gap against position next day If gap > SL level → exit at open, don't hope
Price stalls between entry and 20 SMA Trail SL to breakeven after 5 days
Price reaches 20 SMA fast (2-3 days) Exit 70% (fast moves often overshoot — let 30% ride)
New BB Trap forms in opposite direction Consider closing and reversing
Market-wide crash/violent move All positional bets are off — exit and reassess

Nifty Daily Theta — Sell Premium Every Day with Hedge

Core Philosophy

Sell a little theta every single day. Not once a week — EVERY DAY. Small ₹8-15 credit, hedged with a protective long leg, repeat 200+ times a year. The grind builds the fund steadily. No single day can drain the account because every trade is a defined-risk spread.

What This Is

A daily credit spread on Nifty that fires Mon-Wed (skip Thu/Fri). Each trade sells a far-OTM option and buys an even further-OTM hedge. Maximum loss is the spread width minus the credit — known at entry, capped, no unlimited risk.

This is NOT: - ❌ Naked selling (unlimited risk) - ❌ Weekly-only (too slow for compounding) - ❌ Directional betting (you profit from TIME, not direction)

This IS: - ✅ A hedged credit spread EVERY trading day - ✅ Defined max loss (spread width) - ✅ Theta collection as primary income - ✅ 200+ trades/year → steady compounding

Rules

Entry (Every Mon, Tue, Wed after 1 PM)

  1. Check VIX — must be below 22 (don't sell in high volatility)
  2. Check 20 SMA on Nifty daily chart - Above 20 SMA → sell Bull Put Spread (bullish-neutral) - Below 20 SMA → sell Bear Call Spread (bearish-neutral)
  3. Sell the strike 300 points OTM from spot
  4. Buy the strike 400 points OTM (100pt spread = your hedge)
  5. Target = nearest expiry with 1-4 DTE (days to expiry)
  6. Credit filter = must collect ≥ ₹3 net

The Hedge (Why You Can't Bleed)

Sell 300 OTM → collect premium
Buy  400 OTM → pay premium (this is your HEDGE)
Net credit = sell - buy = ₹8-15

Max loss = spread width (100) - credit (₹10) = 90 points × 75 = ₹6,750
Max profit = credit = ₹10 × 75 = ₹750

You can NEVER lose more than ₹6,750 per trade.
Even if Nifty crashes 1000 points, your loss is capped.

Why This Doesn't Drain the Account

The Math of Safety

Per trade:
  Max profit: ₹750 (if expires worthless)
  Max loss: ₹6,750 (if Nifty moves 300+ pts against you in 1-3 days)
  Risk:Reward = 9:1 (you risk ₹9 to make ₹1)

BUT:
  Win rate = 93%
  Expected value = (0.93 × ₹750) - (0.07 × ₹6,750)
                 = ₹697.5 - ₹472.5
                 = +₹225 per trade

  8 trades/month × ₹225 = ₹1,800/month per lot
  On ₹50,000 capital = 3.6% monthly = 43% annualized

Why 93% Win Rate Is Real

  • 300 OTM means Nifty needs to move 300 points in 1-3 days to hit your strike
  • A 300-point Nifty move in 3 days = ~1.2% — happens but rarely
  • VIX < 22 filter removes the most volatile periods
  • 50% profit booking means you exit early, reducing exposure time

Worst Case Scenario

If you hit max loss 3 times in a row:

3 × ₹6,750 = ₹20,250 loss
But you'd need Nifty to move 300+ points AGAINST you 3 times in a row
in a 1-3 day window — this essentially never happens when VIX < 22

What This Strategy Is

A weekly Nifty credit spread system that sells OTM hawa (time value) every week with a defined-risk hedge. Each trade targets ₹8-15 points of premium with max risk capped at ₹25-40 points. The edge comes from theta decay — time works FOR you, not against you.

This is NOT: - ❌ Naked option selling (unlimited risk) - ❌ Directional betting (you don't need to predict up/down) - ❌ A get-rich-quick system (target is 3-5% monthly, not 50%)

This IS: - ✅ Defined-risk credit spreads (max loss known at entry) - ✅ Theta collection (time decay = your profit) - ✅ Repeatable weekly (4-6 trades per month on Nifty) - ✅ Properly hedged (every short leg has a protective long leg)

Strategy Design

The Trade: Bull Put Credit Spread (Bullish-neutral) OR Bear Call Credit Spread (Bearish-neutral)

You pick ONE side per week based on trend, then sell a credit spread on that side.

Bull Put Spread (use when trend is UP or sideways)

Sell OTM Put (closer to spot) → collect premium
Buy  further OTM Put (protection) → caps downside risk
Net = Credit (you receive money upfront)

Bear Call Spread (use when trend is DOWN or sideways)

Sell OTM Call (closer to spot) → collect premium
Buy  further OTM Call (protection) → caps upside risk
Net = Credit (you receive money upfront)

The Math (why this works)

Example: Nifty at 24,000, VIX 16, Bullish bias

Sell 23,900 PE (100 OTM) → receive ₹18
Buy  23,800 PE (200 OTM) → pay ₹8
Net credit: ₹10 per share = ₹500 per lot (50 qty)

Max profit: ₹500 (if Nifty stays above 23,900)
Max loss: ₹4,500 (if Nifty drops below 23,800 = 200 pts)
Risk:Reward = 1:9 (risk ₹9 to make ₹1)

But probability of profit ≈ 70-75% (Nifty staying above 23,900 for 3 days)
Expected value = (0.72 × ₹500) - (0.28 × ₹4,500) = ₹360 - ₹1,260 = -₹900

Wait — that math looks negative. That's because the raw probability-adjusted EV on a SINGLE trade can be negative when the spread is tight. The real edge comes from:

  1. Entry timing — entering after 1 PM Monday means 2.5 days of theta, not 4. The theta curve is steeper near expiry
  2. VIX filter — only trading when VIX is 13-22 (not too low = no premium, not too high = too risky)
  3. Direction filter — aligning with 20 SMA trend removes ~30% of losing trades
  4. 50% profit booking — not holding to zero. Booking at 50% doubles your win rate

Realistic monthly expectation (based on NK's 15-17% Hedge 17 benchmark): - 4 trades/month × ₹500 average profit = ₹2,000/month per lot - On ₹50,000 capital (spread margin) = 4% monthly = 48% annualized - Even at 60% win rate: (0.6 × ₹500 × 4) - (0.4 × ₹4,500 × 0.5) = ₹1,200 - ₹900 = +₹300/month net (conservative)

Open=Low Intraday Strategy (NSE 200)

Every morning at 9:30, scan NSE 200 stocks. If a stock's Open price = its Low (stock opened at the day's lowest point and never went below), buy ₹10,000 worth. Hold until 1 PM or SL hit (close below the first 15-min candle low).

Core Philosophy

Open=Low means the stock opened at the absolute bottom of the day. Every tick after the open is ABOVE the entry price. This is a strong bullish signal — the entire market day is working in your favor from the first second.
