Trade Review: Arbitrage 2.0 — 2.3-Year Backtest (2024-01 → 2026-05)

Run Details

  • Strategy: Arbitage 2.0 (default rules — ₹10 basis, spread ≤ basis, ₹2,000 spread value, 100 vol each leg)
  • Period: 2024-01-15 → 2026-05-15 (573 trading days, ~2.3 years)
  • Capital: ₹5,00,000
  • Data: NSE daily bhavcopy (FO + CM ZIP archives)
  • Output dir: results/2yr3_2024-01_2026-05/

> ⚠️ Why not 3 years? NSE's public bhavcopy archive (nsearchives.nseindia.com) only serves files back to January 2024. All 2023 dates return HTTP 404. This is a hard wall on NSE's side, not a script bug. 2.3 years is the max achievable window without an alternative data source (paid historical data vendor, or NSE's separate historical-data product).

Key observation

P&L is remarkably even across years (₹1.5L / ₹1.6L / ₹1.6L) despite very different win rates (54% / 45% / 61%). This is the signature of an asymmetric-payoff strategy — the edge is in win/loss size, not hit rate. 2025 had the worst win rate but still produced the middle P&L because losers were small.

Clustering concern

The single worst month (2025-05, −₹27.9K) accounts for ~70% of the entire max drawdown (₹40,149). That's one bad month, not a structural failure. The other negative months are small (−₹3K to −₹7K). If you exclude May 2025, max DD drops to ~₹12K (2.4%).

What was May 2025? Worth investigating — likely a sharp rally that pushed many current futures through spot simultaneously (forcing exits at a loss across the book at once). This is the strategy's known vulnerability: a strong broad-market rally compresses all backwardation structures at the same time.

Interpretation — Is the Edge Real?

The strong case ✅

  1. PF 2.68 over 276 trades is robust — not a small-sample fluke. 276 trades is statistically meaningful.
  2. Consistent across 3 different years with different win rates but stable P&L → the edge is structural (payoff asymmetry), not regime-dependent.
  3. Max DD only 4.94% on 94.56% return → return/DD ratio of 19.1×. Exceptional.
  4. Losses are bounded (max −₹10K) by the spread structure. No tail risk.
  5. 20 of 28 months positive (71%) — consistent monthly edge, not lumpy.

The caveats ⚠️

  1. No costs modeled. Add brokerage (~₹40/round-trip per lot) + STT + slippage (1-2 ticks × 2 legs). On 276 trades × ~₹100-150 all-in cost = ₹27K–₹41K drag. Net PF drops to ~2.4–2.5. Still strong, but worth coding in.
  2. Single worst month = 70% of max DD. May 2025 cluster risk: a broad rally can hit all open positions at once. Position limit (11 max) helps, but correlation spike is the real risk.
  3. Survivorship: bhavcopy reflects stocks listed on that day. Stocks that delisted mid-period aren't in the error term. Minor effect here.
  4. Look-ahead in volume filter? The 100-contract volume filter uses same-day close volume — in live trading you'd screen on prior-day or real-time volume. Minor, but worth noting.
  5. No regime filter. The strategy trades in all conditions. May 2025 suggests a "broad-market momentum" filter (skip when Nifty 50 is up >X% over 5 days) could cut the cluster losses.

Next Iteration Questions

  1. Cost-adjusted re-run — add ₹100/trade all-in cost. Does PF stay above 2.0?
  2. V2.1 on same window — stricter filters (₹20 basis, 2× ratio, 500 vol, 7-21 DTE). Compare trade count and PF.
  3. Spread/basis ratio filter — the worst losers had spread/basis > 45%. Test a spread ≤ 0.4 × basis filter.
  4. Regime overlay — skip entries when Nifty 50 5-day return > +3%. Does it kill the May 2025 cluster?
  5. PAGEIND case study — why 91.7% WR? Replicate those entry conditions as a refined filter.
  6. Live validation — the backtest is now solid enough to justify a small live paper-trade run (1-2 lots) for 2-3 months to confirm execution assumptions.

Verdict

The edge looks real and persistent across 2.3 years and 3 distinct yearly regimes. PF 2.68 with bounded downside and 71% positive months is a legitimate statistical signal, not noise. The main risks are (a) unmodeled costs and (b) correlation-spike drawdowns during broad rallies. Both are addressable.

Recommendation: Proceed to cost-adjusted re-run + V2.1 comparison before committing live capital. If PF stays above 2.0 after costs, this is a deployable strategy at the tested ₹5L scale.
