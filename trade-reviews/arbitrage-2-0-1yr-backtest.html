What the Numbers Say

The Good

  • PF 2.5 at 51% win rate → asymmetric payoff. Average winner is roughly 2.3× average loser. The strategy doesn't need to be right often — it needs to win big when right.
  • 4.68% max DD on 36.85% return → return/drawdown ratio ≈ 7.9. Anything above 3 is considered strong; 7.9 is excellent.
  • Max 11 concurrent positions → manageable on ₹5L. Roughly ₹45k cushion per slot.

The Caveats

  • Single 1-year sample. 2025–26 is one regime. Could be a trending year, low-VIX year, or specific breadth cycle that flatters backwardation structures.
  • 51% win rate is barely above coin-flip. The edge lives entirely in the payoff asymmetry, not in prediction accuracy. If avg win/avg loss compresses, PF collapses fast.
  • No slippage / brokerage modeled in the headline numbers (need to confirm from trades.csv columns — current schema shows raw pnl). Real-world PF will be lower after costs.
  • Survivorship: bhavcopy reflects listed stocks on each day. Delistings during the year may not be captured — could mildly inflate results.
  • No regime filter. Strategy fires whenever structure appears. In high-VIX / trending-down regimes, backwardation can deepen (current future keeps falling below spot) before reverting — that's where the 4.68% DD came from.

Open Questions for Next Iteration

  1. What does V2.1 look like on the same year? Stricter filters (₹20 basis, 2× ratio, 1.5% of spot, 500 vol, 7–21 DTE) will cut trade count sharply but should lift win rate. Worth running.
  2. Cost-adjusted PF. Add realistic brokerage (₹20/order flat + 0.0001% on turnover) and slippage (1-2 ticks per leg). If PF drops below 1.5, the edge is marginal.
  3. Regime split. Break the 132 trades by month — are 80% of profits concentrated in 2-3 months? If so, the strategy may be regime-dependent, not structural.
  4. Multi-year. Minimum 3 years (2023–26) before treating 36.85% as baseline expectation.
  5. Drawdown anatomy. What caused the ₹27,237 DD? Was it one bad trade, a cluster during a specific event (budget, global shock), or spread widening across many positions at once?
