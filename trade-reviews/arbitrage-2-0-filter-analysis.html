Filter Analysis: How to Lift Win Rate on Arbitrage 2.0

Filter Analysis: How to Lift Win Rate on Arbitrage 2.0

Methodology

Pulled all 276 trades from the 2.3-year backtest (results/2yr3_2024-01_2026-05/trades.csv), computed per-trade features, then sliced winners vs losers and tested filters. This is in-sample optimization — these filters will look better than they'll trade live. Treat as hypotheses to validate out-of-sample, not as proven improvements.

What Separates Winners from Losers

Feature Winners (n=144) Losers (n=132) Signal
Holding days 10.0 6.0 🟢 Strong — winners need time to converge
Basis % of spot 1.94% 1.56% 🟢 Strong — bigger relative gap = more room
Basis/Spread ratio 2.09 1.93 🟡 Mild
Spread (₹) 53.0 31.5 🔴 Counterintuitive — wider spreads win more (more premium to capture)
Absolute basis (₹) 95.0 56.9 🟡 Misleading — see below
Spread value (₹) 5,843 5,800 ⚪ No difference
Rank on day 1.51 1.71 🟡 Mild — top-ranked slightly better

Two surprising findings (read these carefully)

1. Absolute basis (₹) is a TRAP. Higher ₹ basis → lower win rate (₹20 → 51%, ₹50 → 49%). Why? A ₹50 basis on a ₹3000 stock (1.7%) behaves very differently from ₹50 on a ₹500 stock (10%). The percentage matters, not the rupee amount. High-₹-basis stocks are often high-priced stocks where 1.5% is "normal" — not an edge.

2. Wide spreads win MORE often, not less. Counter to intuition. Winners have wider spreads (₹53 vs ₹32). Explanation: a wide spread means more premium to capture before convergence — the trade has more "meat on the bone." Don't filter spreads DOWN; the existing spread ≤ basis rule is sufficient.

The Core Trade-off (the honest part)

Every filter that lifts win rate cuts trade count. This is the central tension:

Filter Trades Win Rate PF Net P&L ₹/yr
Baseline 276 52.2% 2.68 ₹4,72,785 ₹2,05,559
Combo G: basis%≥1.0 + ratio≥2.0 77 55.8% 4.54 ₹2,51,083 ₹1,09,167
Combo A: basis%≥1.5 + ratio≥2.0 42 64.3% 6.94 ₹2,11,778 ₹92,077
Combo B: basis%≥1.5 + ratio≥2.5 31 67.7% 10.30 ₹1,83,540 ₹79,800

You can push win rate to 68% (Combo B) — but you give up 60% of your annual P&L. PF goes from 2.68 to 10.30 (beautiful), but you only trade ~13 times a year and make ₹80K instead of ₹2L.

This is not a free lunch. It's a choice between two valid strategies: - Baseline = max absolute returns, lower win-rate psychology - Combo B = max win rate + PF, but capital-efficient boring

My Recommendations (3 tiers)

Tier 1: The "Free Wins" — apply these without thinking

These filters lift PF meaningfully with minimal trade-count loss:

--min-basis-pct 1.0    # Basis ≥ 1.0% of spot (cuts 89 trades, lifts PF 2.68→3.07)
  • Drops to 187 trades, WR 52.2%→54.5%, PF 2.68→3.07
  • Removes the "thin-edge" trades where basis was ₹10 on a ₹2000 stock (0.5%)
  • This is a no-brainer. The ₹10 absolute floor is too loose for high-priced stocks.

Tier 2: The "Sweet Spot" — if you want better quality of life

Combo G: --min-basis-pct 1.0 --max-spread-to-basis 0.5 (basis/spread ratio ≥ 2.0)

Metric Baseline Combo G
Trades 276 77
Win rate 52.2% 55.8%
PF 2.68 4.54
EV/trade ₹1,713 ₹3,261
Net/yr ₹2.06L ₹1.09L
Worst trade −₹9,925 −₹9,925
  • Doubles EV per trade (₹1.7K → ₹3.3K)
  • PF jumps 2.68 → 4.54
  • Trade frequency drops from ~10/month to ~3/month — less screen time, fewer decisions, fewer mistakes
  • This is what I'd actually trade live. The psychological edge of "I lose less than half as often" matters for discipline.

Tier 3: The "Sniper" — only if capital-constrained, not time-constrained

Combo B: --min-basis-pct 1.5 --max-spread-to-basis 0.4 (basis/spread ratio ≥ 2.5)

Metric Value
Trades/yr ~13
Win rate 67.7%
PF 10.30
EV/trade ₹5,921
Worst trade −₹4,680
Net/yr ₹79,800
  • Only fires when the structure is textbook: basis ≥ 1.5% of spot AND spread is ≤ 40% of basis
  • 2 in 3 trades win. Losers are small (worst −₹4.7K)
  • But: 13 trades/year is too few for most traders. Capital sits idle. Use this only if you (a) have limited capital and want max hit-rate per deployment, or (b) run it as a secondary scan alongside a more active variant.

What I'd Actually Do

Run two parallel instances of the strategy with different risk allocations:

Bucket Filter Capital Rationale
Core (70%) Combo G (basis%≥1.0, ratio≥2.0) ₹3.5L Workhorse — 3 trades/month, PF 4.5, pays the bills
Sniper (30%) Combo B (basis%≥1.5, ratio≥2.5) ₹1.5L High-conviction — fires rarely, ~70% WR, "sure things"

This gets you ~5-6 trades/month blended, a combined PF likely ~5-6, and capital stays active. The sniper bucket catches the best setups; the core catches the volume.

Filters I Tested That DON'T Work

Save yourself the time — these looked promising but failed:

Filter Result Why it fails
Absolute basis ≥ ₹50 WR drops to 49.3%, PF 1.63 High-₹ basis = high-priced stocks where % is small
Spread ≤ ₹10 WR 60% but only 95 trades, misses big winners Cuts the "meaty" wide-spread trades that pay the most
Rank 1 only Marginal WR lift, loses 35% of trades Ranking already captures most of the signal
Holding-days cap Can't filter on entry — only known post-trade Not actionable as an entry filter

Critical Caveat: In-Sample Bias

Every number above is in-sample. I found these filters by looking at the same 276 trades they're tested on. In live trading, expect: - Win rates to regress ~3-5 percentage points toward baseline - PF to compress ~20-30% - Combo B's 67.7% WR will likely be ~62-64% live; Combo G's 55.8% will be ~53-55%

To validate properly: 1. Walk-forward test: Optimize filters on 2024 data only, then test on 2025-26 (out-of-sample). I can run this if you want. 2. Paper trade 3 months: Run Combo G live on 1 lot, compare actual WR to backtest WR. 3. Don't over-optimize: The jump from baseline → Tier 1 is robust. The jump from Tier 2 → Tier 3 is where overfitting risk lives. Be suspicious of PF > 5.

Reproducing the Filters

All filters are CLI flags already in backtest_future_arbitaage.py:

# Tier 1 (free win)
python3 backtest_future_arbitaage.py --start 2024-01-15 --end 2026-05-15 \
  --min-basis-pct 1.0 --out-dir results/tier1

# Tier 2 (sweet spot)
python3 backtest_future_arbitaage.py --start 2024-01-15 --end 2026-05-15 \
  --min-basis-pct 1.0 --max-spread-to-basis 0.5 --out-dir results/tier2

# Tier 3 (sniper)
python3 backtest_future_arbitaage.py --start 2024-01-15 --end 2026-05-15 \
  --min-basis-pct 1.5 --max-spread-to-basis 0.4 --out-dir results/tier3

Note: --max-spread-to-basis is the inverse of the ratio. Ratio ≥ 2.0 = --max-spread-to-basis 0.5. Ratio ≥ 2.5 = --max-spread-to-basis 0.4.
