Volume Analysis: Using VPA Principles to Improve Arbitrage 2.0 Win Rate

The Question

> Can we apply the "volume reveals smart money intent" principle from NK's VPA video to improve trade selection in Arbitrage 2.0?

The Answer (short version)

Yes — but not the way you'd expect. The VPA video teaches "effort vs result" (high volume + weak price = trap). That exact pattern doesn't apply to arbitrage because we're not trading breakouts. But the underlying principle — volume reveals liquidity and intent — maps to a different filter: the volume balance between the two futures legs.

The strongest single volume filter is the curr/next volume ratio. When the next-month future (the leg we sell) has decent volume relative to the current-month (the leg we buy), win rate jumps from 52% to 66%.

The key insight: Volume RATIO, not absolute volume

The absolute volume of either leg doesn't separate winners from losers. What matters is the balance between the two legs:

> Curr/Next volume ratio = current FUT volume ÷ next FUT volume

  • Low ratio (≤10): Both legs are actively traded → smooth entry and exit → higher win rate
  • High ratio (>10): Next-month future is illiquid relative to current → hard to exit the sold leg → lower win rate

This is the VPA principle translated to arbitrage: volume reveals whether the market is genuinely supporting both sides of your trade, or whether you're stuck in an illiquid leg.

Why This Works (the mechanism)

The VPA connection

NK's VPA video teaches: volume reveals smart money intent. In a BB Trap, high volume + price failure = institutions selling into the breakout.

In arbitrage, the translation is:

> Volume balance between legs reveals whether the market genuinely supports both sides of your spread, or whether you're trapped in an illiquid position.

What high vol_ratio means in practice

When curr_vol / next_vol > 10:

  • The current-month future is actively traded (easy to buy)
  • The next-month future is illiquid (hard to sell, wide bid-ask)
  • You can enter easily but struggle to exit the sold leg
  • Market makers widen spreads on the illiquid leg → you get worse fill on exit
  • The "convergence" you're betting on may be distorted by illiquidity, not genuine price discovery

What low vol_ratio means

When curr_vol / next_vol ≤ 10:

  • Both legs have genuine market participation
  • Entry and exit fills are tight
  • The spread you're trading reflects real price discovery, not stale quotes
  • Convergence happens cleanly — the gap closes because of genuine market mechanics, not because one leg is stuck

> This is the arbitrage equivalent of VPA's "volume confirms the move." Here, volume balance confirms the trade is executable.

The Honest Catch

1. In-sample bias (same warning as before)

These filters were found using the same 276 trades they're tested on. Expect regression:

  • WR 61.5% → likely 57-59% live
  • PF 5.83 → likely 4-5 live

2. Trade count drops

The best combo (39 trades) = only 17 trades/year. That's 1.4/month. For active traders this feels too slow. But EV per trade is ₹4,532 — each trade is worth ~2.6× a baseline trade.

3. The mechanism test was inconclusive

I tested whether high-ratio trades have larger |curr_move - next_move| (which would prove the illiquidity hypothesis). The result was no significant difference (24.3 vs 26.4 points). This means the win-rate lift might be from selection bias (filtering out inherently weaker stocks) rather than execution quality. The filter still works empirically, but the reason it works may be different than I hypothesized.

4. Volume data is entry-day only

We're using the volume on the day of entry. In live trading, you'd screen on prior-day volume or real-time volume. Minor discrepancy.

Implementation

Add to the backtester

The vol_ratio filter requires a new CLI flag. Currently backtest_future_arbitaage.py has --min-next-volume but not a ratio filter. To add:


# In StrategyRules dataclass:
max_vol_ratio: float = 999.0  # curr_vol / next_vol

# In passes_entry_rules():
vol_ratio = curr.volume / nxt.volume if nxt.volume > 0 else 999
...
and vol_ratio <= rules.max_vol_ratio

CLI: --max-vol-ratio 10
