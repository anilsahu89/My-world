Concept: Survivorship Bias (the Wald Lesson)

Concept: Survivorship Bias (the Wald Lesson)

Definition

Survivorship bias is the logical error of drawing conclusions from only the things that survived a process, while ignoring those that did not (because they're invisible). The classic example is the Abraham Wald WWII aircraft-armor story.

The Wald Story

During WWII, the Allies examined returning bomber aircraft and plotted bullet-hole locations, planning to add armor where holes were densest. Statistician Abraham Wald pointed out the flaw: the armor should go where there are NO holes. The planes with holes in those places didn't make it back — they crashed. The returning planes were the survivors; the data was missing exactly the failures that mattered most.

The Trading Application

From Ravindra Elicherla (EP-73): "Study the trades that failed and the accounts that blew up, not just the winners. That's the Wald lesson."

In markets, survivorship bias appears everywhere:

Survivor data (visible)The missing failures (invisible)
"Top 10 options strategies" listsStrategies that blew up accounts and were abandoned
Successful trader interviewsThe 95% who quit after losing their capital
Stocks currently in Nifty 50Stocks that were delisted/expelled (their returns drop out of indices)
"I made 50% on this trade" postsThe same trader's losing trades they didn't post
Backtests on today's NSE 500Companies that went bankrupt/delisted during the backtest period

Why It's Dangerous for Rita's System

  1. Backtest bias: If a backtest uses today's stock list (e.g., current Nifty 500), it omits companies that failed during the period — making returns look better than reality. Rita's data/nse200_symbols.csv and data/nse500_symbols.csv are current lists → potential survivorship bias in every backtest run on them.
  2. Strategy-selection bias: Reading only about strategies that worked for successful traders hides the ones that looked identical but ruined accounts.
  3. Confirmation bias amplifier: We remember winning trades and forget losers → overconfidence in a strategy's edge.

How to Counter It

  • Ask "what's missing from this sample?" before trusting any return figure.
  • Track every trade, wins and losses, in the paper-trade trackers — Rita already does this (CSVs in papertrades/). Keep the losers visible.
  • Use point-in-time universes for backtests where possible (the stock list as it was on each date, not today's). Flag this as a backtest-accuracy improvement.
  • Study blow-ups explicitly. Read about strategies/accounts that failed, not just successes.
  • In the Arbitrage 2.0 DRREDDY post-mortem approach: do a write-up on every loss, not just wins.

Specific Risks in Rita's Current Data

  • data/ohlcv_daily.csv covers 2024-01 to 2026-07 for currently listed stocks. Stocks delisted during this window are absent → any backtest (BB Trap, Open=Low, base-after-decline) over-estimates returns slightly.
  • The NSE 500 / Smallcap 250 lists fetched today won't include names expelled since 2024.
  • This is a known, accepted limitation — flagged here so it isn't forgotten. Magnitude is usually small over 2.5 years but non-zero.

Mental Model Summary

> When you only count the survivors, you learn the wrong lesson. Armor the places with no bullet holes.
