Concept: Regime Mapping

Concept: Regime Mapping

Definition

Regime mapping is the discipline of classifying the current market state (regime) BEFORE selecting a strategy. The strategy follows the regime — never the other way around. From Ravindra Elicherla (EP-73): "Classify the regime first. The strategy follows the regime."

It is the top-level filter that sits above every individual strategy in the system. Without it, a good strategy applied in the wrong regime becomes a losing strategy.

The Four Core Regimes

RegimePrice behaviorVolatilityBest-fit strategies
UptrendHigher highs/lowsExpanding then coolingBull put spreads, covered calls, cash-secured puts (closer strikes)
Range / ConsolidationSideways, mean-revertingLow & stableWide credit spreads, covered calls (high delta), Iron Condors
Volatile / ChoppyWhipsaw both directionsHigh (VIX elevated)Wider spreads smaller size, or stand aside; avoid naked premium
DowntrendLower highs/lowsExpandingCash-secured puts at lower strikes (paid to wait), reduce/stop call-selling, raise cash

> The VIX level and trend are the fastest regime proxy: low+falling VIX = uptrend/range; rising VIX = transition; high VIX = volatile/downturn risk.

The Rule

> No regime = no trade. If you cannot clearly place the market into one of the four boxes, the default action is stand aside. Discipline over activity.

This directly counters the most common retail mistake: forcing a favourite strategy into every market condition.

How to Determine the Regime (checklist)

  1. Index trend — Nifty above/below its 20 & 50 SMA? (Daily + Weekly)
  2. VIX level & direction — VIX < 15 and falling = calm; VIX > 20 and rising = stress
  3. Market breadth — advances vs declines; are leaders or laggards up?
  4. BB structure — BB squeeze (range) vs walk (trend) vs trap (reversal)
  5. Recent failure rate — if your last 3-4 trades of a type failed, the regime likely shifted against that type

Why It Matters

  • A bull put spread is a high-win-rate strategy in an uptrend and a serial loser in a downtrend. The strategy didn't change — the regime did.
  • Most strategy "failures" are actually regime misapplications. Regime mapping catches this before entry.
  • It is the single highest-leverage addition to Rita's system: it costs nothing and filters out the worst trades.

Backtest Candidates

  1. Regime-gated Daily Theta — does skipping credit spreads when Nifty < 50 SMA + VIX rising improve PF beyond 1.77?
  2. VIX threshold for regime — is VIX 18 / 20 / 22 the cleanest regime cutoff for credit-spread skipping?
  3. Covered-call regime filter — does disabling covered calls in index downtrends reduce assignment-during-drawdown risk?

Limitations

  • Regime classification has lag — by the time a downtrend is obvious, much of the damage is done. Leading indicators (VIX spike, breadth thrust) help but aren't perfect.
  • Overlays with S/R and VIX — not a fully independent signal.
  • Subjective element: "range" vs "choppy" can be hard to distinguish in real time.
