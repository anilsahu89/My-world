Overbought / Oversold (OB/OS)

Definition

  • Overbought: A stock has been excessively bought by retailers → correction likely
  • Oversold: A stock has been sold by all parties (retailers, FII, DII, promoters) → flat bottom

> OB is easier to trade. OS is harder — stock can stay oversold for years.

How to Identify OB/OS

Using Hilega Milega (Primary Method)

OB Trading Rules

  1. HM strength above 86 (monthly/weekly) → OB → correction coming
  2. Double-check: plot trendline at HM's highest point → when strength nears trendline = resistance
  3. For surety: check day timeframe strength near 91–92
  4. If OH (Open=High) condition appears → feel free to take trade
  5. For intraday: use 91–92 on RSI but wait for strength line to reverse before entry

OS Trading Rules

  1. HM strength below 15 (monthly/weekly) → OS → shows flat line
  2. Stock can stay OS for years — don't jump in just because it's below 15
  3. Wait for the flat line to show a curve → only then take trade
  4. Entry = when oversold condition starts reversing (curve in strength line)

RSI-14 Counter-Intuitive Rule

> If any stock is above 70 RSI on weekly/monthly, that stock can double in some months. 70 RSI ≠ overbought in NK's system.

This contradicts traditional RSI usage — NK's OB/OS is defined by HM, not standard RSI levels.

Demand and Supply

  • High volume + low price = Supply is more, demand is less
  • Low volume + high price = Supply is less, demand is more
  • Use RSI-based indicator (RSI 14, OB/OS 70/30) on TradingView for additional confirmation
