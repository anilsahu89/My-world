Nifty Premium Mill — Weekly Hedged Theta Collection

Core Philosophy

> Don't hunt 100 points in one trade. Collect 8-15 points, 4-6 times per month, every month.

> Small premiums, properly hedged, repeated relentlessly. The fund grows slowly but steadily — like a mill grinding grain.

What This Strategy Is

A weekly Nifty credit spread system that sells OTM hawa (time value) every week with a defined-risk hedge. Each trade targets ₹8-15 points of premium with max risk capped at ₹25-40 points. The edge comes from theta decay — time works FOR you, not against you.

This is NOT:

  • ❌ Naked option selling (unlimited risk)
  • ❌ Directional betting (you don't need to predict up/down)
  • ❌ A get-rich-quick system (target is 3-5% monthly, not 50%)

This IS:

  • ✅ Defined-risk credit spreads (max loss known at entry)
  • ✅ Theta collection (time decay = your profit)
  • ✅ Repeatable weekly (4-6 trades per month on Nifty)
  • ✅ Properly hedged (every short leg has a protective long leg)

Strategy Design

The Trade: Bull Put Credit Spread (Bullish-neutral) OR Bear Call Credit Spread (Bearish-neutral)

You pick ONE side per week based on trend, then sell a credit spread on that side.

Bull Put Spread (use when trend is UP or sideways)


Sell OTM Put (closer to spot) → collect premium
Buy  further OTM Put (protection) → caps downside risk
Net = Credit (you receive money upfront)

Bear Call Spread (use when trend is DOWN or sideways)


Sell OTM Call (closer to spot) → collect premium
Buy  further OTM Call (protection) → caps upside risk
Net = Credit (you receive money upfront)

> ⚠️ Never sell when VIX > 22. High VIX means fat premiums but also high gap risk. Wait for VIX to cool.

Direction Selection (which side to trade each week)

Check Nifty on daily chart with 20 SMA:

  • Above 20 SMA + VIX stable/falling → Bull Put Spread (bullish-neutral)
  • Below 20 SMA + VIX rising → Bear Call Spread (bearish-neutral)
  • Chopping around 20 SMA → Pick the side with higher premium (usually puts, since markets have an upward drift)

Entry Timing

  • Monday or Tuesday (after 1:00 PM) — gives 2-3 full days of theta decay
  • Avoid Monday morning (weekend gap noise)
  • Don't enter on Wednesday (not enough time for theta to work)
  • Never on Thursday (expiry day = too late)

The Math (why this works)

Example: Nifty at 24,000, VIX 16, Bullish bias


Sell 23,900 PE (100 OTM) → receive ₹18
Buy  23,800 PE (200 OTM) → pay ₹8
Net credit: ₹10 per share = ₹500 per lot (50 qty)

Max profit: ₹500 (if Nifty stays above 23,900)
Max loss: ₹4,500 (if Nifty drops below 23,800 = 200 pts)
Risk:Reward = 1:9 (risk ₹9 to make ₹1)

But probability of profit ≈ 70-75% (Nifty staying above 23,900 for 3 days)
Expected value = (0.72 × ₹500) - (0.28 × ₹4,500) = ₹360 - ₹1,260 = -₹900

Wait — that math looks negative. That's because the raw probability-adjusted EV on a SINGLE trade can be negative when the spread is tight. The real edge comes from:

  1. Entry timing — entering after 1 PM Monday means 2.5 days of theta, not 4. The theta curve is steeper near expiry
  2. VIX filter — only trading when VIX is 13-22 (not too low = no premium, not too high = too risky)
  3. Direction filter — aligning with 20 SMA trend removes ~30% of losing trades
  4. 50% profit booking — not holding to zero. Booking at 50% doubles your win rate

Realistic monthly expectation (based on NK's 15-17% Hedge 17 benchmark):

  • 4 trades/month × ₹500 average profit = ₹2,000/month per lot
  • On ₹50,000 capital (spread margin) = 4% monthly = 48% annualized
  • Even at 60% win rate: (0.6 × ₹500 × 4) - (0.4 × ₹4,500 × 0.5) = ₹1,200 - ₹900 = +₹300/month net (conservative)

Risk Management (The Non-Negotiables)

Rule 1: Max Loss Per Trade = Spread Width × Qty

  • If spread is 100 pts × 50 qty = ₹5,000 max loss
  • This is KNOWN at entry. No surprises.

Rule 2: Stop Loss = Short Strike Touch

  • If Nifty touches your sold strike → EXIT. Don't hope.
  • The spread is hedged, but don't wait for max loss. Cut at 70-80% of max loss.

Rule 3: Never Average a Losing Spread

  • If a spread goes against you → exit. Don't sell more.
  • "Averaging down" on credit spreads is how accounts blow up.

Rule 4: VIX > 22 = No New Trades

  • High VIX = premiums look tempting but gap risk is real
  • Wait for VIX to drop below 20 before resuming

Rule 5: Max 2 Open Positions at Any Time

  • Don't over-leverage. The mill grinds slowly for a reason.
